🔴 VERY IMPORTANT — Tense-building memory map
Think of imparfait and futur simple as their own separate systems.
Conditionnel is the hybrid: it borrows the stem from the future and the endings from the imparfait.

Imparfait = nous-stem + imparfait endings (-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient)
present nous form − -ons

Futur simple = future stem + future endings (-ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont)

Conditionnel = future stem + imparfait endings (-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient)

Memory shortcut:
Imparfait = its own stem + its own endings
Futur simple = its own stem + its own endings
Conditionnel = FUTURE STEM + IMPARFAIT ENDINGS

Example with pouvoir:
je pouvais = imparfait → pouv- + -ais
je pourrai = futur simple → pourr- + -ai
je pourrais = conditionnel → pourr- + -ais

1. Il y a + completed past event

2. Commencer à + infinitive

When another verb follows commencer, French normally requires à before the infinitive.

J’ai commencé à apprendre le français. ✅
J’ai commencé apprendre le français. ❌

More examples:
Je commence à travailler. = I’m starting to work.
Elle a commencé à parler français. = She started speaking French.
Nous avons commencé à manger. = We started eating.
3. Regular vs. irregular verbs — quick reference

Regular verbs follow a predictable conjugation pattern for their verb group. Common examples:
parler = to speak
aimer = to like/love
travailler = to work
étudier = to study
regarder = to watch
manger = to eat
finir = to finish
choisir = to choose

Common irregular verbs do not follow the normal pattern consistently, so important forms must be learned separately:
être = to be
avoir = to have
aller = to go
faire = to do/make
pouvoir = to be able to / can
vouloir = to want
venir = to come
devoir = to have to / must
prendre = to take
boire = to drink

Important: regular vs. irregular applies across French conjugation generally, not only to the futur simple. A verb may be irregular in different ways in different tenses.
4. Aller à + place

aller à is the normal pattern for “to go to” a place.
J’irai à l’Alliance Française. = I will go to Alliance Française.
Je vais à l’école. = I am going to school.
Je vais à Paris. = I am going to Paris.
Nous allons au restaurant. = We are going to the restaurant.

Remember: à + le = au and à + les = aux.
5. à vs. en vs. au for places

à → cities and many specific places:
à Paris, à Seattle, à l’école, à l’Alliance Française

en → most feminine countries:
en France, en Malaisie, en Allemagne

au → most masculine countries:
au Canada, au Japon, au Portugal

Example: Je vivrai et travaillerai au Canada.
8. Direct vs. indirect object pronouns — why they are called that

A direct object connects to the verb directly, with no preposition in between:
Je vois ma mère.
Structure: verb → object
Because there is no à or other preposition before ma mère, it is direct.

An indirect object connects to the verb through a preposition, usually à:
Je parle à ma mère.
Structure: verb → à → object

Pronoun pattern:
Direct → le / la / les
Indirect (à + person) → lui / leur

Examples:
Je regarde le film. → Je le regarde.
Je vois mes amis. → Je les vois.
Je parle à mon père. → Je lui parle.
Je téléphone à mes parents. → Je leur téléphone.

Important: the pronoun goes before the conjugated verb.
Je le regarde.
Je lui parle.
10. pendant vs. depuis vs. jusqu’à vs. pour

These are easy to mix up, so choose them by meaning rather than translating every English “for” with pour.

pendant = for/during a duration
J’ai pratiqué le français pendant quinze minutes.
Je travaillerai chez moi pendant une demi-heure.

depuis = since/for, when something started in the past and is still continuing
J’étudie le français depuis septembre 2025.

jusqu’à = until / up to a point in time or place
Je travaillerai jusqu’à 19 heures.

pour is often used for purpose or destination, but do not automatically use it for every English “for.”
Je vais au bureau pour travailler. = I’m going to the office to work.

Quick contrast:
pendant deux heures = for two hours
depuis deux heures = for the last two hours and still continuing
jusqu’à 19 heures = until 7 p.m.
11. rentrer — return / go back home

Je suis rentré chez moi. = I came/went back home.
Je rentrerai chez moi. = I will come/go back home.

This is a useful verb to make automatic because literal English translations like je came chez moi or je tournerai chez moi do not work.

Could: two different French meanings

English “could” can mean two different things in French.

1. Past ability → Imparfait

= I could study late when I was a student.
Here, pouvais describes a real ability in the past.

2. Present/future possibility → Conditionnel
Je pourrais sortir ce soir.
= I could go out tonight.
Here, pourrais means it is a possibility / hypothetical option.

Memory question:
Real ability in the past? → pouvais
Possibility now or later? → pourrais

RED — être + adjective vs passé composé

Important: J’étais fatigué is NOT passé composé.

j’étais = I was → imparfait of être
fatigué = tired → an adjective

Je suis fatigué. = I am tired.
J’étais fatigué. = I was tired.

The tense is carried by suis → étais. The adjective fatigué describes the subject.
But “fatiguer” is also a real verb:
fatiguer = to tire / to make tired.

Its passé composé uses avoir:
J’ai fatigué mes jambes. = I tired out my legs.
Le voyage m’a fatigué. = The trip tired me out.

Therefore:
J’étais fatigué. = I was tired. → être in imparfait + adjective
J’ai fatigué... = I tired... → passé composé of the verb fatiguer
Core rule:
Every normal verb can be put into passé composé. Most use avoir; some movement/change verbs and reflexive verbs use être.

But a past idea is not automatically passé composé. French can use être + adjective to describe a past state.

Depuis + present — “have been doing”

When something started in the past and is still continuing now, French normally uses the present tense + depuis:

J’étudie le français depuis neuf mois.
= I’ve been studying French for nine months.

J’habite ici depuis deux ans.
= I’ve been living here for two years.

Movement verbs — similar meanings, useful contrasts

These verbs overlap a lot. Learn them in contrasts instead of as isolated vocabulary.
Verb Core idea French example English
venir to come Elle est venue chez moi. She came to my place.
revenir to come back Elle est revenue hier. She came back yesterday.
rentrer to go/come back home; return inside Je suis rentré chez moi. I went home / came back home.
retourner to return / go back to a place Je suis retourné à Paris. I went back to Paris.
arriver to arrive Je suis arrivé à huit heures. I arrived at eight.
aller to go Je suis allé au bureau. I went to the office.
sortir to go out / leave an enclosed place Je suis sorti hier soir. I went out last night.
partir to leave / depart Je suis parti à huit heures. I left at eight.
Best memory pairs:

venir ↔ aller = come ↔ go
revenir ↔ retourner = come back ↔ go back
arriver ↔ partir = arrive ↔ leave
rentrer = return home / go back inside
sortir = go out
